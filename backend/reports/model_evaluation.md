# Model Evaluation

_Generated by `python -m app.ml.training`. Every metric is computed from the supplied dataset; none is hand-entered._

- Dataset: `project1_supply_chain_demand.csv` — 4,536 cleaned rows, 28 SKUs, 2026-01-01 to 2026-06-29
- Established SKUs: 25 · Newly launched (cold start): 3 (SKU-2000, SKU-2001, SKU-2002)

## 1. Validation design

**Rolling-origin (expanding-window) backtest.** Each fold trains on every day up to its origin, then forecasts the next 14 days from that single origin — the same act a planner performs. Test windows are contiguous and non-overlapping, together covering the last 70 days of the panel.

| fold | train through (origin) | test from | test to | horizon (days) | training rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fold_1 | 2026-04-20 | 2026-04-21 | 2026-05-04 | 14 | 2750 |
| fold_2 | 2026-05-04 | 2026-05-05 | 2026-05-18 | 14 | 3100 |
| fold_3 | 2026-05-18 | 2026-05-19 | 2026-06-01 | 14 | 3450 |
| fold_4 | 2026-06-01 | 2026-06-02 | 2026-06-15 | 14 | 3800 |
| fold_5 | 2026-06-15 | 2026-06-16 | 2026-06-29 | 14 | 4150 |

**Cold-start fold** (`cold_start`, reported separately): origin `2026-06-24`, horizon 5 days, SKUs SKU-2000, SKU-2001, SKU-2002. New SKUs only, 7 days of history at the forecast origin. Reported separately — the sample is small and these errors are not comparable with the established-SKU folds.

### Why not a random split

1. **Leakage through rolling features.** `roll_mean_28` for a given day is built from its 27 neighbours. Under a random split most of those neighbours sit in the training set, so the model is graded on days whose own inputs already encode the answer.
2. **It measures the wrong task.** A planner never interpolates a missing Tuesday between two known days; they stand on the last day of real data and look forward. Only a chronological split scores that.
3. **It is optimistic by construction.** Training on June to predict February hides drift, so the reported error would be better than production error.

## 2. Primary metric: WAPE

```
WAPE = sum |actual - forecast| / sum actual
```

- **MAPE was rejected**: it divides by actual demand, so one quiet day on SKU-1008 (minimum 29 units/day) dominates the average, and it penalises over-forecasting far more heavily than under-forecasting — the wrong asymmetry for a stockout tool.
- **RMSE was rejected as the primary**: it is driven by the 34 demand spikes (|z| > 4) found during profiling, and chasing those would degrade the everyday forecast the planner actually uses. It is still reported.
- **WAPE is unit-free**, so 28 SKUs spanning 85–536 units/day aggregate honestly, and it reads as "we are off by X% of the volume we actually shipped".

Origin `2026-06-24`, when each new SKU had 7 observed days. Only 15 forecast points across 3 SKUs, so these figures are indicative, not a reliable estimate. They are reported to make the cold-start penalty visible rather than to claim accuracy.

For the selected model, WAPE on cold-start SKUs is **0.2158** versus **0.1721** on established SKUs (+25% relative). This gap is exactly why the UI marks these SKUs as low confidence.

**The cold-start fold picked a different winner: `moving_average_28`.** That is mechanically unsurprising — with 7 days of history `lag_14` is missing and the weekday index is almost entirely the category prior, so the pooled model has little to work with while a trailing mean stays robust. Production therefore serves cold-start SKUs from `moving_average_28` and everything else from `hist_gradient_boosting_ratio` (`ColdStartRouter`). The evidence is thin — 15 forecast points — so the routing is deliberately biased toward the *simpler* model for the SKUs we know least about, which is the safe direction to be wrong in.

## 7. Limitations

- **Six months of history, one seasonal cycle.** The panel covers 2026-01-01 to 2026-06-29. There is no second year, so annual seasonality, promotions and holiday effects cannot be learned or validated. Only the weekly cycle is supported by the data.
- **No open purchase orders in the dataset.** `units_received` is a record of past deliveries, not a forward order book. Risk is therefore scored on stock on hand. An estimate of inbound units during the lead time is derived from each SKU's observed replenishment cadence and shown beside the score, clearly labelled, but it is deliberately excluded from the score itself.
- **Demand is recorded, not true, demand.** Closing stock is censored at zero on 275 SKU-days; on those days actual customer demand may have exceeded what the ledger shows. The model learns from recorded units sold, which will under-state demand for chronically short SKUs.
- **Cold-start metrics rest on a handful of points.** The three new SKUs provide 12 days each; the cold-start fold scores a few dozen forecasts. It shows direction, not a dependable error rate.
- **Lead time is a static attribute with no variability.** Real replenishment lead times vary; safety stock here covers demand variability only, not lead-time variability, so it is a lower bound on the buffer actually required.
- **No cost data.** Risk is ranked by probability and by days of excess cover, not by margin, holding cost or stockout cost, so it cannot yet rank a high-value SKU above a low-value one at equal risk.
